\begin{abstract}
Exemplar-free class-incremental learners built on a pre-trained ViT, such as
RSIAT, store one mean and one covariance per class and use them only to
re-align a cosine classifier with sampled pseudo-features. We ask whether
the same statistics can also be used directly at inference time.
\emph{Statistics--classifier fusion} (SCF) adds a generative score computed
from the stored statistics to the classifier logits. It uses a label-free
scale and one weight fixed on a single tuning benchmark, needs no training
and stores nothing new. On four benchmarks with three seeds each, SCF raises
final accuracy on all four datasets ($+0.38$ to $+0.67$ points). It raises
average incremental accuracy on ImageNet-R ($+0.32$), ImageNet-A ($+0.85$)
and CIFAR-100 ($+0.20$), all with 95\% intervals above zero, but not on
CUB-200 ($+0.02$). The pre-registered transfer test to the two new datasets
therefore fails. The work started as an attempt to improve RSIAT with
quantum components. Under the same protocol, none of five quantum
placements beat its matched classical counterpart, including a
pretty-good quantum measurement used as the generative score ($-0.04$
against ridge over 12 runs). All results come from a protocol with paired
seeds, all inference heads evaluated on the same network with bit-identity
checks, and decision rules written before each run. Our reproduction of
RSIAT matches all twelve published accuracies of its main table to within
$0.01$.
\keywords{Class-incremental learning \and Exemplar-free \and Pre-trained models

\end{abstract}
