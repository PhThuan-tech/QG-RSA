\begin{abstract}
Exemplar-free class-incremental learning (EFCIL) methods built on pre-trained
transformers keep one mean and one covariance per class and use them only to
re-align a cosine classifier with Gaussian pseudo-features. We show that the
same statistics carry complementary information that the aligned classifier
discards. A training-free fusion of the classifier logits with a generative
score computed from the stored statistics, weighted by a label-free scale and
a single weight chosen on one tuning benchmark, improves the RSIAT baseline
on every held-out run: by $+0.46$ average incremental accuracy (95\% CI
$[+0.28,+0.64]$) when the generative score is a pretty-good quantum
measurement, and by $+0.62$ ($[+0.39,+0.85]$) when it is a closed-form ridge
classifier. The study was designed to test quantum components, and it reports
them under the same rules. Five placements of a quantum kernel or quantum
measurement were evaluated: a steering metric, a separation metric, a
classifier feature map, a stand-alone measurement and a fusion source. None
of them outperformed its matched classical counterpart. We attribute these
outcomes to a measurement protocol with paired replicates, in-run evaluation
of all heads on one network with bit-identity checks, and decision rules
written before each run. Under this protocol an earlier single-run
improvement of $+0.36$ turned out to lie within training noise. We release
the protocol and the logs.
\keywords{Class-incremental learning \and Exemplar-free \and Pre-trained models

\end{abstract}
