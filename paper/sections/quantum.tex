\section{Quantum Components under Controlled Comparison}
\label{sec:quantum}

The project set out to improve RSIAT with quantum components, so we tested
each one against a classical counterpart that receives the same input and
the same budget. All circuits have at most 11 qubits and are simulated
exactly, so they are classically tractable by construction. The question is
therefore not quantum advantage. The question is whether a quantum
inductive bias helps EFCIL. Table~\ref{tab:quantum} summarises the five
placements we tested.

\paragraph{Projected quantum kernel (PQK).}
A feature is projected to $q=8$ angles,
$\boldsymbol\theta(\mathbf z)=\pi\tanh(W\,\mathrm{LN}(\mathbf z)+\mathbf b)$. The
angles are loaded by $R_y$ rotations, followed by two variational layers of
$R_y$ rotations and a ring of CNOTs. Two inputs are compared through their
one-qubit reduced density matrices~\cite{huang2021power}:
$k(\mathbf z,\mathbf z')=\exp(-\gamma\sum_j\|\rho_j(\mathbf z)-\rho_j(\mathbf z')\|_F^2)$.
The bandwidth $\gamma$ is kept within a factor of 10 of the inverse median
distance. The circuit and training details are documented in the project repository.

\paragraph{1--2. PQK as a learned metric.}
Replacing the cosine similarity of the steering loss with the PQK on the
base task (QKSR) changes AIA by $+0.06$. The CI bounds any effect below
$+0.19$. Applying the frozen PQK as an old/new separation metric during the
incremental tasks makes no difference whether it is evaluated on current
or on previous-model features.

The mechanism is visible in the losses. The PQK separation term stays at
about $0.45$ throughout training, and the kernel between prototypes and
new features stays at $0.5$--$0.6$. The adapter, constrained by the
alignment loss, does not reduce the quantum similarity through an
8-dimensional bottleneck.

\paragraph{3. PQK as a feature map for classifier alignment.}
We fitted ridge heads on the CA pseudo-features using PQK features, RFF
features, random-projection features, or QDA, and compared each with the
linear head inside the same run. Every nonlinear head was worse. The PQK
was the worst, and entanglement made it worse still: the variant without
CNOTs scored $-10.4$, against $-17.3$ with them.

Two effects explain this ranking.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{Degenerate pseudo-data.} With 30 images per class, the class
    covariances have rank at most 29. The Gaussian pseudo-features are then
    trivially separable: RFF reaches $100\%$ on held-out pseudo-data but only
    about $67\%$ on test images. Flexible heads therefore memorise.
  \item \emph{Lossy local reduced states.} The PQK sees only 8 projected
    angles and reads only local reduced states. After random CNOT layers,
    information moves into non-local correlations, so the local states
    concentrate towards the maximally mixed state~\cite{thanasilp2024concentration}.
    The measured kernel--target alignment is $0.34$ for the PQK, against
    $0.37$--$0.38$ for the classical maps.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{4--5. Pretty-good measurement.}
On the same stored statistics, the PGM and closed-form ridge are
statistically indistinguishable. In fusion, the ridge source is at least as
good: it is equal on ImageNet-R ($+0.06$ for the PGM) and better on
ImageNet-A ($-0.29$ for the PGM). Two facts explain why.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{Unitary invariance.} A unitary $U$ applied after the encoding
    maps $\psi\mapsto U\psi$ and $\rho_c\mapsto U\rho_cU^{\dagger}$, and leaves every
    $\Tr(E_c\psi\psi^{\dagger})$ unchanged. A variational circuit placed after
    the amplitude encoding is therefore useless for the PGM.
  \item \emph{Least-squares equivalence.} The PGM coincides with the
    least-squares measurement~\cite{eldar2001srm}, so on second moments it is
    a quadratic relative of ridge regression.
\end{itemize}
The only quantum-specific freedom left is the encoding. A nonlinear
two-copy encoding, $(|0\rangle|\psi\rangle+|1\rangle|u\otimes u\rangle)/\sqrt2$
on 11 qubits, lowered the accuracy, because it adds dimensions that the
class statistics cannot support.

\paragraph{Summary.}
In none of the five placements did a quantum component outperform its
classical control, and in two of them it was clearly harmful. The quantum
view remains useful as a description: the PGM gives the fused score a
principled form. Its benefit, however, comes from the generative use of
the stored statistics and not from the measurement being quantum.
