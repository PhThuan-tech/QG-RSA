\section{Discussion and Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

\paragraph{What helped and why.}
The stored class statistics of EFCIL methods are used only to generate
pseudo-features for the classifier. Using them a second time, as a
generative score fused with the aligned classifier, corrects cross-task
errors that the classifier makes on the oldest classes. The rule needs no
training and no exemplars, and its weight transfers across datasets. It
gains $+0.46$ to $+0.62$ AIA on held-out runs. The diagnosis of
Section~\ref{sec:gap} explains the ceiling: about $3.4$ final-accuracy points
of the gap to joint training lie in the representation, which no
inference-time rule can change.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item We study one baseline (RSIAT), two benchmarks and one split
    (B0Inc20). The held-out set has 8 runs: 3 on ImageNet-R and 5 on
    ImageNet-A.
  \item The fusion weight was tuned on ImageNet-R with seed 1993. Its
    transfer to other backbones and other EFCIL methods with stored
    statistics (e.g.\ \cite{mcdonnell2023ranpac,goswami2023fecam}) is untested.
  \item The three-source fusion is exploratory.
  \item Our quantum circuits are small and simulated. The negative results
    concern these specific placements and encodings, and do not cover
    quantum models in general.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Outlook.}
Since the representation is the bottleneck, the natural next step is to
train the adapter so that the stored statistics remain discriminative,
for example with a measurement- or ridge-based loss during the incremental
tasks. The classical twin should be pre-registered as the control from the
start.

\paragraph{Reproducibility.}
Configurations, logs, analysis scripts and pre-registered specifications
are in the project repository under \texttt{exps/}. Every number in this
paper is generated from these files.
