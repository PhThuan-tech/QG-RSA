# Classical EFCIL prior art for "frozen old-class Gaussians in the training softmax + residual subspace for new classes"

Scope: exemplar-free class-incremental learning (EFCIL / NECIL), 2021–2026, with emphasis on pre-trained ViT + adapter pipelines (SLCA / SSIAT / RSIAT). Proposed method under evaluation: during task t, old classes are frozen class-conditional Gaussians (mean + covariance). Their likelihoods for new-task samples enter the softmax normalizer, so new samples are penalized for landing in old-class regions. New classes are also confined to a residual subspace or budget that old classes have not claimed.

Verification legend: **[V]** = title, authors and venue checked this session against a primary or official page. **[V-arXiv]** = checked on arXiv, venue not confirmed. **[BK]** = from background knowledge and not re-verified this session, so check it before citing.

## Q1. Which EFCIL methods use stored per-class Gaussians or covariances while training new tasks, and what do they do with them?

### Takeaway
Storing an old-class mean and covariance and *sampling pseudo-features from it while training the new task* is well established: IL2A (2021), PASS (2021), EFC/EFC++ (2024/25), AdaGauss (2024), and in PTM pipelines MOS and EASE-style feature synthesis. In every case I checked, old-class Gaussians enter training as **inputs** (sampled or analytically integrated pseudo-features that the classifier must label as the old class) or as **post-hoc classifier calibration** (SLCA/SSIAT/RSIAT CA). I found no method that uses frozen old-class Gaussian **log-likelihoods as the old-class logits** in the softmax applied to *new-class samples*. Covariance-aware **inference** (FeCAM, SEED) is the closest match to the "Gaussian log-likelihood as logit" ingredient, but it is not used as a training-time penalty on new samples.

### Cited Findings
- **IL2A, "Class-Incremental Learning via Dual Augmentation"** (Fei Zhu, Zhen Cheng, Xu-Yao Zhang, Cheng-Lin Liu; NeurIPS 2021, pp. 14306–14318) **[V]**. At the end of each stage it stores the mean μ and covariance Σ of every class (a diagonal version is allowed for large dimensions). While training new tasks it draws old-class features from N(μ_k, γΣ_k). As the number of samples M goes to infinity it derives a closed-form upper bound on the expected cross-entropy (Jensen's inequality plus the Gaussian moment-generating function). The result is a CE whose old-class logits are shifted by γ/2·vᵀΣ_k v with v = φ_c − φ_k. The **softmax denominator runs over all seen classes (C_old + C_t)**, and generated and real samples are "jointly fed to the classifier". Loss: L = L_new + α·L_semanAug + β·L_kd, with γ = 2 and α = β = 10. The introduction states that old and new classes "would be easily overlapped in the deep feature space" because of representation bias — [NeurIPS proceedings](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2021/hash/77ee3bc58ce560b86c2b59363281e914-Abstract.html); [full text](https://object.cloud.sdsc.edu/v1/AUTH_da4962d3368042ac8337e2dfdd3e7bf3/ml-papers-txt/NeurIPS/2021/classincremental_learning_via_dual_augmentation__0a55c8c5.txt); [code](https://github.com/Impression2805/IL2A)
- **EFC, "Elastic Feature Consolidation for Cold Start Exemplar-Free Incremental Learning"** (Simone Magistri, Tomaso Trinci, Albin Soutif-Cormerais, Joost van de Weijer, Andrew D. Bagdanov; ICLR 2024) **[V]**. It stores each class mean (updated at the end of every task) and the covariance from the task in which the class first appeared, and samples Gaussian prototypes during later tasks. **PR-ACE (asymmetric CE)** has two terms. (1) CE on current images over **current-task classes only**. (2) CE on a batch that mixes Gaussian-sampled old prototypes with a separate batch of current images, over **all seen classes**. Classes are sampled uniformly, so prototypes take up a growing share of each batch (about 50% at task 2 and about 2/3 at task 3). The paper names "inter-task confusion" and "task-recency bias" as the problems PR-ACE addresses. It does not use the word "intrusion" — [ICLR proceedings](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/874411a224a1934b80d499068384808b-Abstract-Conference.html); [arXiv 2402.03917](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.03917v2)
- **EFC++** (Magistri et al., arXiv 2503.10439, 2025; **[V-arXiv]**, venue not confirmed). It **removes prototypes from backbone training**: backbone training uses only current-task CE plus the EFM regularizer. Old prototypes are then drift-compensated with an EFM-weighted average of current-sample drift. After training, the classifier is re-balanced with the backbone frozen, using sampled prototypes plus current features and CE over all classes. The paper argues that backpropagating the joint loss on new data causes extra drift along directions important for old tasks — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.10439v3)
- **AdaGauss, "Task-recency bias strikes back: Adapting covariances in EFCIL"** (Grzegorz Rypeść, Sebastian Cygert, Tomasz Trzciński, Bartłomiej Twardowski; NeurIPS 2024) **[V-arXiv; NeurIPS acceptance stated by the authors and lab page]**. It adapts both the **means and the covariances** of old classes after each task. It attributes task-recency bias to **dimensionality collapse** during training and adds an anti-collapse loss. It reports results both from scratch and from a pre-trained backbone — [arXiv 2409.18265](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.18265?context=cs); [CVC LAMP NeurIPS 2024 list](https://lamp.cvc.uab.es/tag/neurips-2024/)
- **FeCAM** (Dipam Goswami, Yuyang Liu, Bartłomiej Twardowski, Joost van de Weijer; NeurIPS 2023) **[V]**. It keeps the **backbone frozen** after the first task and classifies with a Bayes classifier that uses per-class prototypes plus covariance (Mahalanobis distance). It argues that Euclidean distance is suboptimal because CL feature distributions are heterogeneous. The Gaussians are **used only at inference**; no training loss involves them — [NeurIPS](https://papers.nips.cc/paper_files/paper/2023/hash/15294ba2dcfb4521274f7aa1c26f4dd4-Abstract-Conference.html)
- **SEED, "Divide and not forget: Ensemble of selectively trained experts in Continual Learning"** (Grzegorz Rypeść et al.; arXiv 2401.10191; ICLR 2024 **[BK — venue not confirmed by search]**). Each expert models every class as a full-covariance Gaussian, and a sample is classified by Gaussian likelihoods with a softmax temperature (T = 3 beats T = 1). For each new task it **fine-tunes only the expert whose latent Gaussians for the new classes overlap least** with existing ones (minimum-overlap selection beats the alternatives). Using diagonal covariance lowers accuracy (53.5%) — [arXiv 2401.10191](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2401.10191)
- **ADC, "Resurrecting Old Classes with New Data for Exemplar-Free Continual Learning"** (Goswami et al.; CVPR 2024) **[V]**. It perturbs current samples adversarially toward old prototypes in order to estimate drift and move the prototypes. It handles drift, not the placement of new classes — [CVF](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2024/papers/Goswami_Resurrecting_Old_Classes_with_New_Data_for_Exemplar-Free_Continual_Learning_CVPR_2024_paper.pdf)
- **LDC, "Exemplar-free Continual Representation Learning via Learnable Drift Compensation"** (ECCV 2024) **[V]**. It learns a projector that corrects prototype drift, including rotation and scaling. This is also a drift method — [ECCV poster](https://eccv2024.ecva.net/virtual/2024/poster/1940); [arXiv 2407.08536](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.08536)
- **FeTrIL, "Feature Translation for Exemplar-Free Class-Incremental Learning"** (arXiv 2211.13131; WACV 2023 **[BK — venue not confirmed]**). It freezes the extractor and builds old-class pseudo-features by translating new-class features: f_new − μ_new + μ_old (mean only, no covariance). A linear SVM or FC layer is then trained on these pseudo-features plus real ones — [paperswithcode](https://cs.paperswithcode.com/paper/fetril-feature-translation-for-exemplar-free); [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2211.13131v2)
- **SSIAT, "Semantically-Shifted Incremental Adapter-Tuning is A Continual ViTransformer"** (Yuwen Tan et al.; CVPR 2024) **[V]**. It uses one shared adapter on a frozen ViT, estimates the semantic shift of old prototypes without old samples, and **samples features from the stored (shifted) prototype distributions to retrain a unified classifier after the task** (post-hoc, SLCA-style). The extended arXiv version is retitled "Continual Adapter Tuning with Semantic Shift Compensation…" — [arXiv 2403.19979](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.19979v2); [CVPR supplement](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2024/supplemental/Tan_Semantically-Shifted_Incremental_Adapter-Tuning_CVPR_2024_supplemental.pdf)
- **MOS, "Model Surgery for Pre-Trained Model-Based CIL"** (Zhou et al.; arXiv 2412.09441; AAAI 2025 **[BK]**). It replays class statistics generated from a Gaussian distribution to remove classifier bias, again post-hoc — [arXiv 2412.09441](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2412.09441)
- **EASE, "Expandable Subspace Ensemble for PTM-based CIL"** (Zhou et al.; CVPR 2024) **[V]**. It adds one lightweight adapter per task to form task-specific subspaces, and synthesizes old-class features in new subspaces (semantic-guided prototype complement) because old classifiers are incompatible with the new spaces — [CVF](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2024/html/Zhou_Expandable_Subspace_Ensemble_for_Pre-Trained_Model-Based_Class-Incremental_Learning_CVPR_2024_paper.html)
- **Adversarial Pseudo-replay for EFCIL** (Honda; WACV 2026) **[V]**. It generates adversarial pseudo-replay images while training the new task and learns a per-class transfer matrix that recalibrates old covariances — [CVF WACV 2026](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/WACV2026/papers/Honda_Adversarial_Pseudo-replay_for_Exemplar-free_Class-incremental_Learning_WACV_2026_paper.pdf)
- **KMDR, "Knowledge Modeling and Distribution Refinement in Pre-trained Model for CIL"** (Springer LNCS chapter 2025; **[V] partial: title only**). It names "feature overlap", where new-class embeddings collide with existing clusters in a frozen PTM space. It stores each class as a compact GMM and trains a projector with an intra-class variance-suppression loss — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-032-10486-1_8)
- **Background-knowledge entries [BK], not re-verified this session.** PASS (Zhu et al., CVPR 2021: prototype augmentation with an isotropic Gaussian radius plus self-supervision, with old prototypes in the CE over all classes). SSRE (Zhu et al., CVPR 2022: PASS-style prototypes plus structural reparameterization). SLCA (Zhang et al., ICCV 2023: slow learner plus post-hoc classifier alignment by sampling from per-class Gaussians). RanPAC (McDonnell et al., NeurIPS 2023: random projection plus a Gram/ridge classifier, no per-class covariances). ACIL (Zhuang et al., NeurIPS 2022) and DS-AL (AAAI 2024): analytic recursive least squares that stores an autocorrelation matrix, not per-class Gaussians. APER/ADAM (Zhou et al., IJCV 2024): prototype classifier. CPrompt (CVPR 2024) and HiDe-Prompt (NeurIPS 2023): HiDe stores per-class Gaussian statistics to replay pseudo-features for task-identity inference and output-layer alignment.

### Inferences
- In the classical literature, old-class Gaussians enter training **through the input side** (sample from N(μ_old, Σ_old) and require the label "old"). The proposed method enters **through the logit side**: for a new sample x, the logit for old class k is log N(f(x); μ_k, Σ_k), and the softmax normalizer penalizes new samples that score high under old Gaussians. In the population limit both are cross-entropy terms over a joint old+new softmax, but the proposed gradient falls on the **new-class features** and pushes them out of old high-density regions. IL2A/EFC gradients fall on **classifier weights** (and the backbone) to keep old samples correctly classified. This difference is the main novelty lever.
- In a hybrid head (QDA logits for frozen old classes, linear logits for new classes), the "frozen old Gaussian logits" piece is exactly FeCAM's/SEED's Bayes classifier. So the classical twin is "FeCAM-style Gaussian logits for old classes, used *during* training with learnable new-class logits", which is effectively a training-time version of FeCAM combined with the logic of SEED's overlap-minimizing expert selection.
- EFC++ reports that putting old prototypes into the backbone loss *increases* drift. This is evidence a reviewer could raise against the proposed method if gradients from the old-class terms reach the backbone or adapter.

### Gaps
- I could not read the RSIAT main paper or supplement (CVF returned 403), so I could not confirm exactly how RSIAT's training loss treats old classes during incremental tasks. The search snippet for the supplement says CA uses "class-wise Gaussian statistics" post hoc with logit normalization.
- I did not find, and so cannot rule out, a 2025–2026 paper that uses old-class Gaussian log-likelihoods directly as logits in the new-task CE. Searches did not surface one. Before claiming novelty, run a targeted search: "Gaussian discriminant head" + "incremental", "generative classifier class-incremental (van de Ven et al., CVPRW 2021)", "Mahalanobis logits continual training".

## Q2. Which methods reserve feature space for future classes or force new classes into a null space or orthogonal complement? Are any exemplar-free with covariance-weighted subspaces?

### Takeaway
Reserving **feature** space has two main lines: forward-compatible virtual prototypes (FACT, CVPR 2022; NC-FSCIL ETF, ICLR 2023 [BK]) and **PRL (NeurIPS 2024)**. PRL is the closest. It is exemplar-free, squeezes the base embedding to reserve space, and during incremental training pushes new-class features to be **orthogonal to stored old prototypes** in a latent space produced by an undercomplete autoencoder projector. However, it uses **prototypes (means) only, not covariances**, and it trains ResNet-18 from scratch. Orthogonal/null-space methods (GPM, Adam-NSCL, InfLoRA, O-LoRA) operate in **parameter or gradient space**, not in a covariance-weighted class-feature space. I found no exemplar-free method that defines the new-class budget as the residual of the old-class covariance mass.

### Cited Findings
- **PRL, "Prospective Representation Learning for Non-Exemplar Class-Incremental Learning"** (Wuxuan Shi, Mang Ye; NeurIPS 2024) **[V]**. In the base phase it "squeeze[s] the embedding distribution of the current classes to reserve space for forward compatibility with future classes" (Preemptive Embedding Squeezing: intra-class concentration plus "inter-class reserved separation"). In the incremental phase it applies **Prototype-Guided Representation Update**: L_ort = Σ_{x∈B, p_c∈P^{0:t−1}} |⟨P_φ(F_{θ_{t−1}}(x)), P_φ(p_c)⟩|, which makes new features orthogonal to old prototypes. P_φ is an undercomplete autoencoder (linear→ReLU→linear→sigmoid). It adds L_align = MSE(P_φ(F_{θ_{t−1}}(x)), F_θt(x)). The backbone is ResNet-18. The ablation shows PES matters more than PGRU — [NeurIPS proceedings PDF](https://proceedings.nips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/file/01ecd39ca49ddecc5729ca996304781b-Paper-Conference.pdf); [poster](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2024/poster/94608); [slides](https://nips.cc/media/neurips-2024/Slides/94608.pdf)
- **FACT, "Forward Compatible Few-Shot Class-Incremental Learning"** (Da-Wei Zhou et al.; CVPR 2022, pp. 9046–9056) **[V]**. It pre-assigns virtual prototypes, "pretending they are the reserved space" for future classes, and creates virtual instances by instance mixture — [CVF PDF](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2022/papers/Zhou_Forward_Compatible_Few-Shot_Class-Incremental_Learning_CVPR_2022_paper.pdf); [arXiv 2203.06953](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.06953)
- PRL's related work lists: Chaudhry et al. (orthogonal low-rank embedding subspaces on the Stiefel manifold), Saha et al. GPM (SVD bases of representation subspaces, gradient projection), Zhou et al. (virtual prototypes = FACT), and Shi et al. (mimicking an oracle model in the initial phase, CwD) — [PRL PDF](https://proceedings.nips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/file/01ecd39ca49ddecc5729ca996304781b-Paper-Conference.pdf)
- **Miles, "Metric Learning with Expandable Subspace for PTM-based CIL"** (arXiv 2607.17593, 2026; **[V-arXiv] title only**). It projects features into task-specific subspaces to reduce inter-class boundary confusion — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.17593v1)
- **[BK] parameter-space orthogonality, not re-verified this session:** GPM (Saha et al., ICLR 2021), Adam-NSCL (Wang et al., CVPR 2021, null space of the feature covariance of old tasks, i.e. the uncentered input covariance per layer), InfLoRA (Liang & Li, CVPR 2024, LoRA down-projection designed so updates avoid interfering with the old-task input subspace), O-LoRA (Wang et al., Findings of EMNLP 2023, orthogonal LoRA subspaces). NC-FSCIL (Yang et al., ICLR 2023) pre-assigns a simplex ETF of classifier prototypes for all (including future) classes.

### Inferences
- Adam-NSCL and InfLoRA *are* covariance-based (they use the input-feature covariance of old tasks at each layer), but they constrain **weight updates**, are class-agnostic, and must store a matrix per layer. The proposed method constrains **where new classes may place output-feature mass**, weighted by per-class covariance. That is a different object, but reviewers will likely see it as "Adam-NSCL in feature space". Cite it and contrast it explicitly.
- RSIAT's "residual autoencoder-based projector" for aligning old and new distributions looks architecturally descended from PRL's undercomplete-autoencoder projector, and RSIAT's base-task "representation-steering loss that supports adaptation to future tasks" looks like a PRL-PES-style forward-compatibility term. This is unverified because I could not read the RSIAT text. If true, **PRL is the direct classical ancestor of both the baseline and the proposed method**, and the delta must be argued against PRL: covariance rather than mean, likelihood-normalized softmax rather than an |cos| orthogonality penalty, and a pre-trained ViT rather than ResNet-18 from scratch.

### Gaps
- I did not verify NC-FSCIL, GPM, Adam-NSCL, InfLoRA or O-LoRA bibliographies this session (one search for InfLoRA returned nothing).
- I found no exemplar-free method that allocates a residual subspace weighted by old-class covariance energy (for example, the complement of the top eigenspaces of Σ_old pooled across classes). This is a negative result from a limited search (about 15 calls), not an exhaustive one.

## Q3. Is "intrusion" or new-class overlap onto old-class regions (as opposed to feature drift) identified as the main cause of forgetting in PTM-based CIL?

### Takeaway
Overlap and confusion between old and new classes is widely named as *a* cause (PRL, IL2A, KMDR, SEED, EFC's "inter-task confusion"). However, I found **no paper that establishes intrusion, rather than drift, as the dominant cause of forgetting specifically in PTM+adapter CIL** with a measurement protocol. Most PTM work (SSIAT, ADC, LDC, EFC++, AdaGauss, RSIAT's SSCA) frames the problem as drift or recency bias. This gap is an opportunity, provided the project's own diagnostics (SSCA/CA measurement protocol) support it.

### Cited Findings
- PRL: "with the influx of new classes, there are overlaps of the old and new classes in the embedding space that are difficult to discriminate… handling this conflict with only new task data is intractable". Its t-SNE analysis says baseline overlap increases and causes "confusion between the old and new classes". The setting is ResNet-18 from scratch, not PTM — [PRL PDF](https://proceedings.nips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/file/01ecd39ca49ddecc5729ca996304781b-Paper-Conference.pdf)
- IL2A: old and new classes "would be easily overlapped in the deep feature space" (representation bias) — [IL2A text](https://object.cloud.sdsc.edu/v1/AUTH_da4962d3368042ac8337e2dfdd3e7bf3/ml-papers-txt/NeurIPS/2021/classincremental_learning_via_dual_augmentation__0a55c8c5.txt)
- KMDR (PTM-based): "feature overlap" in which new-class embeddings collide with existing clusters in the frozen PTM feature space — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-032-10486-1_8) (search summary only; full text not read)
- EFC: "inter-task confusion" and "task-recency bias", addressed with prototype rehearsal — [arXiv 2503.10439](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.10439v3)
- AdaGauss attributes recency bias to dimensionality collapse, which is a representation cause rather than intrusion — [arXiv 2409.18265](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.18265?context=cs)
- "Causally Sufficient and Necessary Feature Expansion for CIL" (Zhang et al., arXiv 2603.09145, 2026; preprint, no venue): in expansion methods "task-specific features learned from the new task may collide with the old features", which it attributes to spurious correlations and inter-task semantic confusion between similar classes — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.09145)

### Inferences
- The word "intrusion" does not appear to be standard terminology in CIL. The closest terms are "overlap", "collision", "inter-task confusion" and "task-recency bias". Defining intrusion operationally (for example, the mass of new-class samples under the old-class Gaussian density, or the old→new confusion rate with drift held fixed) would be a contribution in itself.

### Gaps
- I found no PTM-specific decomposition of forgetting into drift and overlap components in the literature searched.

## Q4. RSIAT bibliographic details

### Takeaway
Verified: **Jiarui Zhao, Libo Huang, Xiangqi Li, Zhulin An, Chuanguang Yang, Yu Wang, Boyu Diao, Yongjun Xu. "Representation-Steered Incremental Adapter-Tuning for Class-Incremental Learning with Pre-Trained Models." CVPR 2026, pp. 18010–18020** (the page numbers come from the authors' README BibTeX). Code: github.com/zjrzjrz/RSIAT, built on PILOT and SSIAT.

### Cited Findings
- The CVPR 2026 virtual poster page lists the title and the eight authors. The abstract describes one shared adapter for all tasks (no parameter growth), a **representation-steering loss in the base task** that improves discrimination and "supports adaptation to future tasks", and, in incremental tasks, a **residual autoencoder-based projector** that aligns feature distributions between old and new tasks while preserving representation consistency. It reports results on six benchmarks — [CVPR 2026 poster](https://cvpr.thecvf.com/virtual/2026/poster/40170)
- README BibTeX: @inproceedings, CVPR 2026, pages 18010–18020. The code is based on PILOT and SSIAT. Datasets: CIFAR-100, CUB, ImageNet-A, ImageNet-R, OmniBenchmark, VTAB — [GitHub](https://github.com/zjrzjrz/RSIAT)
- The supplement exists on CVF open access, but the fetch returned 403. A search snippet says it describes post-hoc classifier alignment that models class-wise Gaussian statistics and normalizes logits — [CVF supplement](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2026/supplemental/Zhao_Representation-Steered_Incremental_Adapter-Tuning_CVPR_2026_supplemental.pdf)

### Inferences
- From the abstract, RSIAT's SSCA (drift compensation, inherited from SSIAT) and CA (post-hoc Gaussian pseudo-feature classifier alignment) both act **after** or **outside** new-task training. The proposed method's change (old Gaussians inside the new-task training softmax) is therefore orthogonal to RSIAT's components and can be framed as a drop-in loss.

### Gaps
- The main paper PDF and HTML were inaccessible (403), so I could not verify the exact steering loss, the SSCA formulation, or whether any training-time loss uses old-class statistics.

## Q5. Closest classical analogue to "softmax over frozen old-class Gaussian log-likelihoods + learned new-class logits in the training loss", and ranking

### Takeaway
The best classical twin is **IL2A's semanAug (NeurIPS 2021)**: an analytic, full-covariance, joint old+new softmax CE with stored per-class (μ, Σ). **EFC's PR-ACE** is its modern, sampled, asymmetric counterpart. The "Gaussian log-likelihood as logit" ingredient is FeCAM/SEED, and the "residual space for new classes" ingredient is PRL (with FACT before it). The proposed method's distinguishing combination is: (a) old Gaussians used as **frozen generative logits evaluated on new samples** rather than as replayed inputs; (b) a **covariance-weighted** residual budget for new classes rather than mean-only orthogonality or virtual prototypes; (c) a PTM+shared adapter setting. I found no single prior work that combines (a) and (b).

### Ranked: 5 closest works and how the proposed method differs
1. **PRL — Shi & Ye, NeurIPS 2024 [V].** It is the closest in *intent*: exemplar-free, reserves space for future classes, and during new-task training pushes new features away from old-class prototypes. It also uses an autoencoder projector, which is likely RSIAT's ancestor. *Differences:* PRL uses means only with an |inner-product| orthogonality penalty; the proposed method uses full covariance and likelihood normalization. PRL reserves space in the base phase by squeezing; the proposed method uses a residual budget derived from old-class covariance at every task. PRL uses ResNet-18 from scratch; the proposed method uses a ViT+adapter PTM.
2. **IL2A (semanAug) — Zhu et al., NeurIPS 2021 [V].** It is the closest in *loss form*: stored (μ, Σ) per class enter a closed-form expected CE whose normalizer spans old and new classes. *Differences:* IL2A integrates over **old-class pseudo-inputs** to protect old decision boundaries, with gradients reaching classifier weights. The proposed method evaluates **new samples** against frozen old densities so that new features are pushed out of old regions. IL2A has no subspace or budget constraint and was trained from scratch.
3. **EFC / EFC++ — Magistri et al., ICLR 2024 / arXiv 2025 [V]/[V-arXiv].** Gaussian prototype replay with an asymmetric CE (current-only CE plus an all-class CE on prototypes mixed with new data). *Differences:* it samples old Gaussians as inputs, and its EFM regularizer constrains drift, not placement. EFC++ shows that injecting prototypes into backbone training can increase drift, which is a risk to test for in the proposed method.
4. **FeCAM (NeurIPS 2023) [V] / SEED (arXiv 2401.10191; ICLR 2024 [BK]).** These use Gaussian (Mahalanobis) log-likelihood classifiers over full covariance with a softmax over likelihoods (SEED), and SEED selects the expert whose new-class Gaussians overlap least with old ones. *Differences:* Gaussian logits are used only at inference (FeCAM) or for expert selection (SEED). Neither puts frozen old-class likelihoods into the new-task training loss alongside learnable new-class logits, and neither reserves a residual subspace.
5. **FACT — Zhou et al., CVPR 2022 [V]** (and NC-FSCIL, ICLR 2023 [BK]). Forward-compatible reservation of embedding space through virtual prototypes or ETF prototypes. *Differences:* the reservation is fixed in advance and geometric (prototype directions), with no covariance and no old-class likelihood penalty, in a few-shot from-scratch setting. The proposed method derives the reserved budget from what old-class Gaussians actually occupy.

Honorable mentions: Adam-NSCL / InfLoRA (covariance null-space, but in parameter space and class-agnostic) [BK]. SLCA / SSIAT / RSIAT CA (Gaussian pseudo-feature classifier alignment, post-hoc only) [V for SSIAT/RSIAT]. AdaGauss (covariance adaptation plus anti-collapse) [V-arXiv]. ### Cited Findings

### Inferences
- Suggested ablations so the novelty is defensible: (i) replace the old-class Gaussian logits with sampled-prototype replay (IL2A/EFC style) and keep everything else fixed; (ii) replace the covariance-weighted residual budget with PRL's mean-only orthogonality loss; (iii) replace the frozen Gaussian logits with FeCAM inference only (no training term). If the proposed method beats all three on PTM benchmarks, the "logit-side, covariance-weighted" claim holds.
- Analytically, a softmax with frozen QDA logits for old classes and linear logits for new ones is a **hybrid generative–discriminative classifier**. Cite the generative-classifier CL line (van de Ven, Li & Tolias, "Class-incremental learning with generative classifiers", CVPRW 2021 [BK]) as the conceptual ancestor.
